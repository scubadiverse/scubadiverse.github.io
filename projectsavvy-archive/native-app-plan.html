Focus & Flow – Native App with Health Sync

A plan to rebuild your focus app as a real phone app that can read your stress from Apple Health, Samsung Health, Google Health Connect and Garmin – and calm you down automatically.

The honest headline. Your current app is a web page, and web pages cannot read health data from any phone platform – Apple, Samsung, Google and Garmin all block it. The only way to reach that data is a native app installed from the App Store / Play Store. This is the plan for that app.

What it would do

Everything the app does today – focus timer, projects, priority helper, mood check-ins, breathing, rain & hairdryer sounds, water/break/time-out reminders – plus one new power:

Automatic stress rescue. The app reads your live stress / heart-rate data from your watch or phone. When your stress climbs above a level you set, it steps in on its own: a 2-minute time-out, plays rain, and guides you to breathe – exactly what you asked for, without you having to notice or press anything.

Which health sources it connects to

Instead of wiring each one separately, we use one service – Terra – that bundles 500+ devices behind a single connection:

🍎 Apple Health 📱 Samsung Health 🤖 Google Health Connect ⌚ Garmin Connect ＋ Fitbit, Oura, Whoop, Polar…

How it gets built

1

Rebuild the app for phones ~1–2 weeks

Re-create the current app in React Native – one codebase that becomes both an iPhone and an Android app. Same look and features you already have.

2

Add the health connection ~1 week

Plug in Terra to read stress / heart-rate, and wire the "stress too high → time-out + rain + breathe" rule.

3

Test on real phones & publish ~1–2 weeks

Test with a real watch, then submit to the App Store and Play Store. Apple's review of a health app can take several days and sometimes asks questions.

Rough total: about 4–6 weeks of focused work for a first working version.

Honest limitations

  • Not truly real-time. Watches send stress data in chunks after they sync (every few hours, or when you open the health app). The app would check every ~15 minutes – so the rescue comes soon after a stress spike, not the exact second.
  • App Store review. Health apps get extra scrutiny and can take days; occasionally rejected and resubmitted.
  • Ongoing upkeep. A native app needs updates as phones and rules change – it's not "build once, forget."

My recommendation

Start small and cheap to prove it works before spending on everything:

  • Build a rough test app that connects just your own phone's health data (e.g. Apple Health on your iPhone) and triggers the breathe.
  • If the stress-rescue feels right, then do the full build and publish to the stores.

Meanwhile, your free web app keeps working today with the manual "breathe now" button, so you lose nothing while we decide.
